Okay, so this is the BR-220 bracket. Ten-gauge mild steel, one 90-degree bend. First thing, check the job sheet and grab the one-inch V die and the matching punch. Look them over for chips. A chipped die marks every part. Before you change any tooling you lock out the machine, every time, no exceptions. Set the back gauge to the flange length on the sheet. Then the trick most guys miss: always bend a test piece from the same batch first. Steel from different suppliers springs back different. If your test piece comes out at 92, you adjust before you run the order, not after you've scrapped twenty parts. Check it with the protractor, and get your first good part signed off by your lead. And keep your hands out of the light curtain. If it trips, you stop and figure out why.
